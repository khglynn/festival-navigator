Austin, month by month

“See and save shows with links to buy that leverage what we’ve built here… let’s not break relationships and build some special snowflake.”Kevin, 24 Sep

Kevin missed MUNA at Moody Amphitheater on Sat Sep 19. It was announced May 8, on sale May 14, and never sold out: 134 days of notice, and nothing he uses told him. So this page designs two things together: a season (Austin’s shows by month, pick them, tap through to buy) and an alert (a message when an artist he loves is announced here). Every card, stack, head and zoom below is the app’s own code running on the real season file: 425 shows at 15 rooms, read off each venue’s calendar today. Rest the mouse on a card (or hold it) to zoom; tap to pick.

What the app draws today

The season file fed straight into today’s code, unchanged. It already works as a calendar: every month is a dated section, so it becomes a tab, every date a room, every venue a stack. What gives it away as a festival: the tabs are cut to five letters (SEPTE OCTOB NOVEM), every head says the month instead of the date (THU SEPTEMBER · SEP 24), and the show menu offers months, not rooms. The zoom has no buy link and drops the support acts.

What all three share

  1. One Austin, entered like a fest. It sits in the fest switcher. Tabs are the months that have shows, the current month first: SEP OCT NOV DEC JAN FEB MAR APR MAY.
  2. A night is a room. One head per date, the way fest heads read: FRI OCT 16. Under it, today’s venue stacks in doors order. A card is a show; the headliner is its name.
  3. The zoom says how to go. Support acts under the name, then when, where, and Tickets · Ticketmaster as a door (or Door only). A future on-sale rides the same line.
  4. Yours means an artist in your Spotify, or one you picked at any fest. Kevin’s taste here is seeded (below); the green pill on a card is today’s Spotify chip, unchanged.
  5. The spring is thin, and says so. Venues announce two to four months out, so in September the spring is nearly empty. A thin month ends with one quiet line that says why, instead of looking broken. That gap is the alert’s job.

Three doors

The three differ on one thing: what the season opens on, and how the alert reaches Kevin. Each is drawn on a phone from production code, then the alert as it would have landed for MUNA, on MUNA’s real dates.

browse first

A · The month

Opens on this month, at tonight’s room, the way a fest opens on today. A Slack DM the moment a show by one of your artists is found. A show announced this week wears a small NEW on its card for seven days.

Cost: the smallest. Month tabs and date heads are two changes to code that exists; NEW is one corner label. The alert needs a daily job, a taste list and a Slack app.

alert first

B · Yours

A YOURS tab before the months, there only when something matches: every Austin date by an artist you love, soonest first, each in its night room, with the on-sale time under it while it is still ahead. A weekly Slack digest, and a DM only when an on-sale is inside 48 hours.

Cost: medium. A tab that comes and goes (the NOW tab’s code), the on-sale whisper, and on-sale times from Ticketmaster’s API, which none of today’s sources carry.

going out first

C · This week

A THIS WEEK tab first: the next seven nights, everything on. The months follow, without the nights the week already shows. Slack reminds you the day before a show you picked, and the day before an on-sale for yours.

Cost: medium. The week tab is a moving window (it has to roll at midnight); the day-before reminder needs picks the server can read.

The rooms filter

Kevin: “filters for what venues we’re covering will be a nice way to show what we have or don’t.” It lives where the app already hides parts of the week: the fest name opens the show menu. The rooms we read are rows with a check and a count; the rooms we don’t read yet are named plainly under them. Untick a room and its stacks leave; the rest close the gap. It stays open while you tick, because fifteen rooms is a list you edit, not one switch.
